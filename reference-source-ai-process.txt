Product overview
----------------

[Source lines 11538-11540]
Memory Lens is a phone app that watches the world through a camera — your phone's, or a pair of Ray-Ban Meta smart
glasses — and quietly remembers what it saw, so you don't have to. It answers "where are my keys?", warns you before
you walk out without something, and talks you through building things step by step, checking your work as you go.

[Source lines 11547-11549]
Project — A patient building coach. Photograph an instruction sheet (or scan its QR code) and it breaks the page into
steps, then watches you work and speaks up: "Step done. Next: fold the top left corner" — or "Careful, that fold looks
wrong."

[Source line 11553]
Settings — Your AI key, free model choices, light/dark themes, storage and privacy controls.

Object memory, checklists, and retention
----------------------------------------

[Source lines 83-91]
Watch-for rules from the product spec:
- "remember-object": passively track where an object was last seen
- "exit-check": alert when the user leaves without a required object
("don't let me forget my keys before leaving the house")
- "requirement": generalized exit-check — the requirement may be an
object ("keys") or an action ("brushed teeth"); vision tags count
as evidence for actions, object sightings for things
- "checklist": named item lists (gig kit, groceries); items check off
when seen, and leaving/checkout without an item alerts

[Source lines 629-633]
• Requirements & checklists UI — "Don't leave before…" (action evidence via vision tags), named checklists with
comma-separated items, and live per-item progress (○ seen / ✅ packed).
• Retention controls — per-photo delete (memory stays as text), text export, confirm-guarded wipe-today, and one-tap
cleanup enforcing the 500-photo cap plus 30-day pruning via new quota functions in
memory-lens/src/services/photoFiles.ts and delete/detach helpers in memory-lens/src/services/memoryStore.ts.

[Source lines 8455-8458]
• No more invented sightings: the capture engine refuses to analyse "no frame" on real sources, and stops loudly —
with the reason on screen — the first time a real photo can't be checked, instead of storing placeholder memories or
photographing unscreened scenes. Simulation still demos, clearly labelled.
• Wipe today now deletes voice-note audio files too, not just photos.

Project step previews
---------------------

[Source lines 1652-1655]
• Model builder — new memory-lens/src/services/stepModel.ts, pure and dependency-free: parses part quantities ("Leg
x4"), classifies parts into primitives (legs→cylinders, tops→flat boxes, screws→small cylinders, knobs→spheres,
unknown→box), stages them deterministically (done steps back-center, current step in front highlighted, future steps
behind ghosted), and computes a camera-framing radius.

[Source lines 1660-1662]
• Visual encoding, tested — the geometry-args and material mapping lives in partMeshProps() (single source of truth
used by the view), covered by unit tests asserting radii, highlight color/emissive, and ghost transparency per
state.

Camera sources and Android wearable integration
-----------------------------------------------

[Source lines 1736-1739]
Yes, conditionally — any glasses with a camera work if something can get their JPEG frames onto your network as either
a snapshot URL or a WS binary stream. That "something" is the gap: you'd need a small bridge (a companion phone app, a
script polling the glasses' media API, or a wearable relay). Glasses with an open API or accessible photo storage are
bridgeable today with ~30 lines of glue; locked-down glasses with no media access are not.

[Source lines 5888-5893]
What changed: the Android module was stubs that threw "not wired". It now calls the actual DAT 1.0.1 SDK — I read the
API straight from the shipped Maven artifacts rather than from docs or memory: session create/start/stop, permission
status, device list + battery, camera streams at the configured fps/quality (frames converted to JPEG for the existing
pipeline), photo capture, lens-display text, and registration-intent forwarding. Two real bugs found and fixed along
the way: Wearables.initialize isn't idempotent (ALREADY_INITIALIZED on repeat calls), and the eyewear bridge never
connected before streaming.

Voice notes, recaps, and montage
--------------------------------

[Source lines 2893-2902]
- **Mic only (no camera)** — voice notes (tap to record, transcribed,
audio kept) and ambient sampling (short samples become text memories,
audio deleted). Needs `POST /transcribe` on the backend; without it,
voice notes save untranscribed.
- **Recaps** — End-of-day and end-of-week summaries from your text
memories: AI-narrated via `POST /recap` with a backend, counted
locally without one.
- **Cartoon montage** — saved event photos become a cartoon MP4
(`POST /cartoon-montage`, server ffmpeg) with an in-app slideshow
fallback.

[Source lines 2906-2908]
- **POV build recap** — record POV clips while building, then stitch
clips + today’s photos into a sped-up timelapse MP4 (`POST /timelapse`,
server ffmpeg) with an in-app slideshow fallback.

Fast Capture
------------

[Source lines 1177-1181]
Frames flow through a bounded ring buffer (90 frames / 4s) into the
on-device change trigger; each event keeps its pre/post window, and with
a backend configured the representative frames go to `POST /analyze-clip`
for multi-frame analysis (“what happened”), otherwise the trigger frame
is captioned alone.

Layout and accessibility
------------------------

[Source lines 8200-8210]
• One theme system (memory-lens/src/components/theme.tsx): every screen and shared component now reads design tokens —
no hardcoded colors left in the four tabs. Light = soft paper with one solid Meta-blue accent; Dark = deep ink with
a lighter blue that holds contrast. Settings → Look & feel → Match phone / Light / Dark, applied instantly
everywhere, persisted on-device, and it follows the OS when set to Match phone.
• Big, calm, one-job-per-card layout: 20pt-radius cards, 56pt-tall buttons (hard to miss, easy for small hands), 28pt
headings, 15–17pt body text, soft shadows in light mode only, plainer dark surfaces. Tab bar and headers are themed
too, with visible selected states.
• Words a child gets: Memory now opens with "Memory Lens — I keep an eye on your things, so you don't have to."
Sections renamed to plain jobs: "Where should I look?", "Find my things", "Teach me an object", "Just listen (no
camera)", "Start watching" with the big button under it. Project opens with "Let's build it", Fast Capture explains
itself in one line.

[Source lines 11580-11582]
It speaks instead of demanding reading. Step guidance and mistake warnings are spoken aloud, and the newest build
talks you through folds and fixes while your hands are busy. Screens use large text, large touch targets, and plain,
short sentences everywhere.

Everyday use cases
------------------

[Source lines 11597-11609]
You don't need a diagnosis to forget — you need a Tuesday. Normal life is full of small, expensive lapses:

The wallet in yesterday's jacket, the passport before the airport run, the sunglasses on the café table.

"Did I already add salt?" mid-recipe, with the glasses watching the counter.

Assembling furniture at midnight: the app holds the instructions, reads the step, and checks your shelf bracket before
the wall does.

Travel and gig checklists ("gig kit: guitar, cable, picks") verified against what was actually seen packed.

A searchable trail for the little mysteries: where the remote went, which box the cables are in, when the package was
left at the door.
